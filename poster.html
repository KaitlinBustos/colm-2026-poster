The CT-Bench Environment
Game2 players (P-Red, P-Blue) on a 4×4 board; move to a tile by paying a chip of its color
ChipsEach player holds 14 chips of only its own color (+2 green), so trades are needed to cross the other color
Scoring20 points for reaching the goal + 5 points per remaining chip; each player maximizes its own score
Pay-for-PartnerPlayers promise to cover each other's moves; promises are non-binding, so partners can renege
Why Contracts?

As AI agents operate autonomously in multi-agent settings, they must cooperate with other agents and with humans. Cooperation is hard because its costs come early but its benefits come later, creating an incentive to defect.

Human societies solve this principal–agent problem with contracts: observable representations of agreements that enable credible commitment through enforcement of terms.

Can LLM agents use self-negotiated contracts to cooperate with commitment?
Board Types (80 boards)
  • Independent (20): each player can finish alone — tests rule-following without needing cooperation
  • Mutually Dependent (20): neither player can finish without trading — tests finding mutual gains
  • Asymmetric (40): P-Red can finish alone, P-Blue cannot — tests bargaining under power asymmetry

Metrics: Normalized Joint Reward, Gini (inequality), Both Finished, Both Beat Baseline (BBB), Defection.

Self-Negotiated Contracts

Agents negotiate their own contracts, in three forms:

  • Programmatic Trading: chip transfers to cover agreed tiles, auto-executed
  • Natural Language Trading: contract in natural language, an LLM judge enforces it
  • Programmatic Points: promise part of your final score, auto-paid

Negotiation runs up to 8 turns; a judge model (Qwen-3-235B) summarizes agreed terms into a JSON contract that both players must accept. The judge is highly accurate (0.26% error across 1,155 approved moves).

A Programmatic Trading contract: when the receiver steps on the tile, the chip transfer executes automatically; a giver who cannot pay scores 0.

Contract Formation
  • Agents reliably reach agreement: 86% mean acceptance (Haiku-4.5 an outlier at 67%, p<0.001)
  • Representation shapes fairness: Programmatic Points yields winner-takes-all deals on 39% of Asymmetric boards (74% for Qwen models); Programmatic Trading assigns equal tile counts in 49% of cases

Both Finished, Board by Board

Mean Both Finished across all 80 boards and contract types. Programmatic Trading gives small but consistent gains on Mutually Dependent and Asymmetric boards.

Contracts Help the Weaker Player

On Asymmetric boards, contracts primarily benefit the weaker player: under Programmatic Trading, P-Blue's average reward is 68% higher than without contracts (p<0.001), while P-Red shows no significant gain — the stronger player fails to exploit its bargaining position.

When Contracts Backfire

On Mutually Dependent boards, natural-language contracts underperform having no contract at all: Both Finished is 46% vs 61% (No-Contract) vs 79% (Programmatic Trading).

Mechanism — over-anchoring: contracts formed are similar, but Natural Language Trading suppresses the follow-up trading still needed to finish (1.4 vs 3.0 proposals per game). When declining to trade, models cite their contract 94% of the time, vs 74% under Programmatic Trading.

Contracts Change Behavior on the Board

Contracts can improve outcomes. On boards where models fail to reach their goals without a contract, adding a Programmatic Trading contract lets every model complete its route.

Beyond Self-Play

Pairing GPT-4.1 with Haiku-4.5 (strong–strong) and Qwen-3-30B (strong–weak) on Mutually Dependent boards: under Programmatic Trading, the strong model lifts the weak model at its own expense while joint reward stays high — efficient cooperation survives large capability gaps.

Without contracts, GPT-4.1 scores 0 as P-Red against Qwen-3-30B, which honors only 6% of its promises (Haiku-4.5 honors 100%). Programmatic Trading enforces the transfers and resolves this.

Takeaways
  1. The way contracts are represented and enforced matters: contracts that compile to programs consistently outperform natural-language ones
  2. Self-negotiated contracts can improve cooperative outcomes beyond what regular trading achieves
  3. Agents struggle to achieve mutually beneficial outcomes from asymmetric starting points — structured commitment closes part of this gap
